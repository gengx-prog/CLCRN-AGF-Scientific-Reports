\title{Evaluating adaptive graph fusion for short-range weather forecasting with conditional local convolution}
\author[1,*]{Xinchen Geng}
\affil[1]{University of Southern California, Los Angeles, CA 90089, USA}
\affil[*]{Corresponding author: gengx@usc.edu}
\keywords{Weather forecasting; Adaptive graph learning; Conditional local convolution; Reproducibility}
\begin{abstract}
I evaluate an adaptive graph-fusion (AGF) extension to the Conditional Local Convolution Recurrent Network (CLCRN), originally proposed by Haitao Lin et al., on four coarse-grid WeatherBench-derived tasks. The encoder gates a graph-aggregated projection against a node-wise residual projection before the CLConv recurrent backbone. I newly train 40 primary models (two variants, four tasks and five seeds) and six mean-fusion ablations, each for 100 epochs in FP32. Training, validation and testing consistently include finite targets equal to zero; checkpoints are selected using validation MAE alone. Across five training seeds per variant, AGF reduces relative-humidity MAE by 2.38\% and RMSE by 3.29\% (Benjamini--Hochberg adjusted $q=0.00604$ and $0.000653$, respectively, across eight primary tests). The other six comparisons do not establish a difference at $q<0.05$. I additionally report latitude-weighted scores, horizon-specific errors and sampled missing-input diagnostics. The evidence concerns training-seed variability on one fixed split and does not establish competitive skill against large-scale global weather models.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Numerical weather prediction integrates physical governing equations and
remains central to operational forecasting~\cite{bauer2015quiet}.
WeatherBench and the ERA5 reanalysis have also enabled systematic evaluation
of data-driven weather models~\cite{rasp2020weatherbench,hersbach2020era5}.
Recent global models, including FourCastNet, Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, and
probabilistic forecasters, address multivariate medium-range prediction at
much finer spatial resolution than small graph-recurrent
benchmarks~\cite{pathak2022fourcastnet,bi2023accurate,lam2023learning,price2025probabilistic}.
Compact models remain useful for controlled experiments on training cost,
task-specific adaptation, and reproducibility, provided that their narrower
forecasting scope is stated explicitly.

This study builds on the Conditional Local Convolution Recurrent Network
(CLCRN), proposed by Haitao Lin et al.~\cite{lin2022conditional}. CLCRN and its
Conditional Local Convolution (CLConv) operator are contributions of that
prior work. The present study adds an AGF input extension and evaluates
its effects under matched conditions. CLConv uses geographic information to construct
location-dependent kernels. The present data occupy a regular
$32\times64$ latitude--longitude grid embedded on the sphere, rather than
an irregular sampling grid. Geometry-conditioned local aggregation is the
published backbone contribution. The question studied here is whether
augmenting that backbone with a learned graph yields a reproducible benefit
for 12-hour forecasting (Supplementary Fig.~S1).


Learned adjacency and gating are established ideas. Diffusion and
graph-convolutional recurrent networks~\cite{li2018diffusion,seo2018structured}
and spatio-temporal graph convolution~\cite{yu2018spatio} provide fixed-graph
baselines. Graph WaveNet, AGCRN, and learned graph-structure
models~\cite{wu2019graph,bai2020adaptive,shang2021discrete} motivate topology
estimated from data. Attention-based forecasting further demonstrates the
value of learned dependencies~\cite{vaswani2017attention,liu2023spatio}.
The adaptive-graph and fusion design is inspired by ASTTN~\cite{feng2022adaptive},
which combines representations from geographical and learned graphs.
Here I use a compact graph aggregation and node-wise residual projection
before CLConv, without ASTTN's Gumbel graph masking or spatial--temporal
multi-head attention.
I do not claim the ingredients of the AGF encoder as individually novel.
It is a compact combination of node embeddings, directed adjacency,
top-$k$ aggregation, and gated residual mixing used to test an incremental
change to an existing geometry-conditioned model.

The contribution of this study is the AGF input extension to the published
CLCRN framework and its controlled empirical evaluation: identical
data, training schedules, and budgets for CLCRN and CLCRN-AGF; five seed
replicates; uncertainty estimates and multiplicity correction; and a
traceable connection between results, checkpoints, and figures. I also
report component ablations, diagnostic analyses, and negative or inconclusive
results. The manuscript follows a Methods--Results--Discussion structure
so that the implementation and evaluation conventions are specified before
the numerical findings.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Data, chronological split, and normalization}\label{sec:data}
I use the four WeatherBench-derived tasks adopted by
CLCRN~\cite{lin2022conditional,rasp2020weatherbench}: 2-m temperature (K),
relative humidity (percentage points), 10-m wind components ($u,v$;
m\,s$^{-1}$), and total cloud cover (fraction). The two wind components are
predicted together; the other tasks are trained separately. Hourly data
from 1 January 2010 through 31 December 2018 are supplied on a regular
$32\times64$ latitude--longitude grid ($N=2{,}048$).

Each example contains 12 hourly inputs followed by 12 hourly targets.
Windows start 24 hours apart, yielding 3,286 examples. Chronological,
unshuffled train/validation/test partitions contain 2,300/329/657 examples.
Thus successive examples do not share a 24-hour input--target window.
For each physical variable (and separately for each wind component), the
normalization mean and standard deviation are computed over the training
input arrays across examples, input times, and nodes. The same statistics
transform inputs and targets. In the frozen implementation, these statistics are computed from the original training-input arrays. Inputs and targets are then cast to float32 before in-place subtraction of the training mean and division by the training standard deviation. This float32-before-standardization order is used for training, validation and test inference and is recorded in the processing-version manifest. Forecast errors are calculated after inverse standardization.

\subsection{Backbone and implementation-aligned AGF encoder}\label{sec:architecture}
The CLConv--GRU encoder--decoder is based on the public CLCRN implementation.
CLConv uses coordinates, geodesic distance, and orientation information to
condition local convolution. These operators and the recurrent update are
retained; AGF augments the encoder input rather than replacing CLConv.
Both local variants use trainable node embeddings, whereas the upstream
reference implementation registers its node embeddings as a fixed buffer.
Thus the control is a matched local CLCRN implementation retaining the
published CLConv--GRU operators, rather than an unchanged reproduction
of every upstream implementation choice.
During training, the autoregressive decoder uses curriculum-based teacher
forcing. At evaluation, it consumes its own previous predictions.

Let $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{bti}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ be the standardized input at
example $b$, time $t$, and node $i$. A linear weather projection and a learned
node embedding, each of dimension 8, form the AGF input
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{bti}=\phi_x(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{bti})\Vert\mathbf{e}^{\mathrm{node}}_i
\in\mathbb{R}^{16}.
\label{eq:agf_input}
\end{equation}
Source and destination embeddings $\mathbf{E}_s,\mathbf{E}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times10}$
define a directed, row-normalized adjacency,
\begin{equation}
A_{ij}=\frac{\exp(\mathbf{e}^{s\top}_i\mathbf{e}^t_j)}
{\sum_{\ell=1}^N\exp(\mathbf{e}^{s\top}_i\mathbf{e}^t_\ell)}.
\label{eq:adaptive_adjacency}
\end{equation}
This adjacency uses the same softmax of source--destination embedding
products as ASTTN (Eq.~8)~\cite{feng2022adaptive}; the graph aggregation
and fusion implementation used here is specified below.
This graph is parameter dependent but does not change with the input
weather sample. The implementation retains the self-edge and the largest
$k-1$ non-self entries, with $k=4$, then renormalizes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}_k(i)=\{i\}\cup\operatorname{top}_{k-1}\{A_{ij}:j\ne i\},
\qquad
\widetilde A_{ij}=\frac{A_{ij}}{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}_k(i)}A_{i\ell}}
\quad(j\in\mathcal{T}_k(i)).
\label{eq:topk_normalization}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{top}_{k-1}$ returns indices. Selection does not exclude
geographically nearby nodes, so the learned edges are potentially long-range
but are not guaranteed to be nonlocal.
Aggregation, projections, and fusion follow the source-code order:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{m}_{bti}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}_k(i)}\widetilde A_{ij}\mathbf{F}_{btj},\\
\mathbf{q}_{bti}&=\mathbf{W}_m\mathbf{m}_{bti}+\mathbf{b}_m,
&\mathbf{r}_{bti}&=\mathbf{W}_r\mathbf{F}_{bti}+\mathbf{b}_r,\\
\mathbf{g}_{bti}&=\sigma\left(\mathbf{W}_g[\mathbf{r}_{bti}\Vert\mathbf{q}_{bti}]
+\mathbf{b}_g\right),\\
\mathbf{z}_{bti}&=\mathbf{g}_{bti}\odot\mathbf{q}_{bti}
+(1-\mathbf{g}_{bti})\odot\mathbf{r}_{bti}\in\mathbb{R}^{8}.
\label{eq:agf_fusion}
\end{align}
Both branches act on the same pre-CLConv input. In particular,
$\mathbf r$ is a node-wise residual linear projection, not a
geometry-conditioned CLConv representation. The encoder receives
$[\mathbf F\Vert\bar{\mathbf x}\Vert\mathbf z]$; the control receives
$[\mathbf F\Vert\bar{\mathbf x}]$. The corresponding input widths are
25 versus 17 for the scalar tasks and 26 versus 18 for wind. There is no
calendar input, periodic encoding, or multi-head attention in the primary
configuration. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the corrected computation.

\subsection{Matched new training and checkpoint selection}\label{sec:training}
Both models use two recurrent layers with 32 hidden units, 8-dimensional
feature and node embeddings, one CLConv propagation view, and 25 geographic
neighbours. Adam uses learning rate 0.01 and $\epsilon=0.001$, batch size 32,
gradient-norm clipping at 5, and MultiStepLR milestones $\{10,20,30,40,50\}$
with factor 0.95. Every run completes 100 epochs; early stopping is disabled.
The curriculum-learning decay parameter is 2,000. All primary and ablation
runs use FP32, with automatic mixed precision and TF32 disabled.
Training and validation MAE are evaluated after inverse standardization,
including every finite target, including physically valid zero values.
Testing uses the same zero-inclusive support. Every epoch is saved atomically;
the earliest epoch attaining the minimum full-validation MAE is selected,
without consulting the test set. Checkpoints retain optimizer, scheduler,
random-number-generator and scaler states to permit interruption recovery.
All trainable parameters are materialized before optimizer construction,
and optimizer membership and gradient coverage are checked explicitly.

The prior archive used a zero-exclusive objective. Its scores are superseded
by the newly trained results reported here. The correction therefore applies
to optimization and validation selection, rather than solely rescoring
historical checkpoints. Code is frozen before training, and per-run manifests
record source hashes, configuration, seed, precision and environment.

The recorded training interpreter is Python 3.13.7, with PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128. GPU information is recorded as ``NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 Laptop GPU, 610.88, 24463 MiB''.


\subsection{Test metrics and replicate-level inference}\label{sec:metrics}
The primary MAE and RMSE pool all finite targets over test examples, the
12 forecast times, nodes, and output components with equal weight:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{a=1}^M|\widehat y_a-y_a|,
\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{a=1}^M(\widehat y_a-y_a)^2}.
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
These are unweighted grid-point metrics, not area-weighted scores. I also
report a latitude-weighted sensitivity analysis, replacing the unit weights
with $w_i=\cos\phi_i$ (latitude $\phi_i$ in radians) and dividing weighted error sums by the sum of weights
over valid entries. This changes the evaluation metric only; it does not
change the training objective or checkpoint selection. For cumulative horizon $h$, errors pool steps
$1,\ldots,h$; exact-step scores use only step $h$. Persistence repeats the
last input observation and is scored by the same test implementation.

Each primary task has five independently trained control and five AGF
replicates. Reusing seed labels does not create matched random streams,
because AGF changes the parameter initialization sequence. I report means,
sample standard deviations, AGF-minus-control differences, two-sided Welch
95\% confidence intervals, and two-sided Welch tests. Benjamini--Hochberg
correction is applied across the eight task/metric tests. The 95\% intervals are marginal, not simultaneous or multiplicity-adjusted intervals; the adjusted $q$ values govern the primary significance statements. A two-sided exact
permutation test of the difference in means provides a sensitivity check
under the null of exchangeable group labels, over the
$\binom{10}{5}=252$ group allocations. This
replaces the previous paired sign-test interpretation. With only five seeds
per group, these intervals characterize training variability on one fixed
split; they do not quantify uncertainty across climates, datasets, or test
periods. A non-significant result is not evidence of equivalence. With five replicates per group, distributional assumptions are difficult to assess; the exact permutation check is also conditional on exchangeability and does not remove this small-sample limitation.

\subsection{Provenance and supplementary experiments}\label{sec:provenance}
The fixed experiment manifest contains 40 newly trained primary models:
control and AGF for four tasks and seeds 2021--2025. Six additional newly
trained mean-fusion models cover temperature and humidity with seeds
2023--2025. Every model completes the same 100-epoch FP32 protocol.
The corresponding control and gated-AGF ablation rows reuse exactly those
three seeds from the primary experiment; they are not separately selected
favourable runs. Without AGF, the entire input augmentation is removed.
Without a learned gate, the fusion is $\mathbf z=(\mathbf q+\mathbf r)/2$.

All accuracy tables, horizon plots, weighted scores and gate diagnostics
are regenerated from this common set of new checkpoints. Missing-input
diagnostics use a documented shared subset. Historical tuning trials and
short screening runs are not used as substitutes for these replicates.
Source and checkpoint hashes, seed-level CSV files, validation-selection
records and figure metadata are retained with the analysis.

The external CLCRN reference means are traceable to Table~2 of the published
AAAI paper~\cite{lin2022conditional}. Temperature and wind scores retain
their displayed units; humidity errors are multiplied by 10 and cloud-cover
errors divided by 10 to match the units used here. These historical values were not
recomputed with the present evaluator. WeatherBench's standard evaluator
uses cosine-latitude weights, whereas the released CLCRN evaluator uses
unweighted means and masks zero targets. I therefore state both conventions
explicitly without assuming that a historical reference shares the corrected
zero-inclusive protocol. The source audit and unit-conversion table are
retained in the accompanying reproducibility materials under \texttt{historical\_context}; the primary table contains only matched scores.


\subsection{Computational scope}\label{sec:cost}
The AGF implementation constructs a dense $N\times N$ similarity matrix before top-$k$ selection, so graph construction has quadratic intermediate memory despite sparse aggregation. CLConv also materializes dense kernel operators. The recorded trainable parameter counts are 39,268 for the scalar control and 80,892 for scalar AGF; wind uses 39,437 and 81,061, respectively. The inherited inference microbenchmark is identified separately as a measurement of historical weights. Its timings are not relabelled as measurements of the newly trained checkpoints.

\subsection{Missing-input robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

Both variants are evaluated on 64 deterministically spaced common test windows. Node-mask ratios 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4 use mask seeds 11, 22 and 33, shared between variants. Masks remain fixed across input hours and signal channels; masked standardized values are replaced by zero, corresponding to the training mean. Masks are averaged within each training seed before summarizing five seeds. These sampled diagnostics (Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}) neither establish full-test robustness nor provide a significance test for robustness differences.

At 40\% masking, mean relative MAE changes from each checkpoint's clean score are Temperature: AGF +465.9\%, control +474.1\%; Relative humidity: AGF +74.7\%, control +73.0\%; Surface wind: AGF +84.7\%, control +85.7\%; Cloud cover: AGF +37.5\%, control +35.5\%. Each checkpoint supplies its own clean-score denominator, and all variants share the evaluated windows and mask seeds. The large temperature degradation reflects this training-mean-imputation stress test and does not demonstrate behaviour under naturally occurring missingness.

\subsection{Computational cost in context}\label{sec:cost_results}

Across the 46 completed runs, the sum of recorded epoch training-plus-validation durations is 56.34 GPU wall-clock hours on a serial single-GPU queue. This sum excludes initialization, final test inference, interruption/restart overhead and supplementary analysis; it is not an end-to-end campaign-duration or energy estimate.

\paragraph{Historical-weight inference microbenchmark.}

The following inherited timing evidence uses archived temperature seed-2021 weights, not the newly trained weights in the accuracy tables. It measures a fixed batch of 32 standardized windows, FP32, 12 inputs and 12 outputs, on an RTX 5090 Laptop GPU after 10 warm-up iterations and 30 synchronized repetitions. Historical control/AGF mean batch latencies were 220.53/205.60 ms (iteration SD 18.53/5.43 ms), with peak allocated memory 333.65/614.50 MiB. This sequential repeated-batch profile does not establish a speed advantage, does not measure new-weight inference, and excludes preprocessing. The historical benchmark data remain separately labelled in the supplied revision package.

\paragraph{Context relative to recent global weather models.}
The scope of CLCRN-AGF is a controlled, task-specific forecast on a
$32\times64$ grid, using 12 hourly inputs to predict the next 12 hours.
Recent global ensemble systems address a substantially broader task
(Table~\ref{tab:large_weather_context}). WeatherNext~3 reports improved
probabilistic skill over WeatherNext~2 for most upper-level targets and
surface fields in its 2024 evaluation, with some exceptions at short lead
times~\cite{rasp2026weathernext3}. FourCastNet~3 reports skill competitive
with GenCast and better than IFS-ENS in its 2020 WeatherBench~2-style
evaluation~\cite{bonev2025fourcastnet3}. These reports do not establish an
accuracy ranking against CLCRN-AGF: the verification years, initial
conditions, spatial resolutions, variables, forecast horizons, spatial
weighting and deterministic versus probabilistic scores differ. In
particular, pressure-level specific humidity is not interchangeable with
the relative-humidity target used here. A numerical head-to-head comparison would
require harmonized data and verification.

\paragraph{Training cost and practical scope.}
The reported training resources also differ substantially. WeatherNext~3
uses about 6.7 wall-clock days per model seed; its two seeds jointly
require about 4.8 TPUv4 chip-years and 8.9 TPU7x chip-years~\cite{rasp2026weathernext3}.
FourCastNet~3 reports three stages: 78~h on 1,024 H100 GPUs, 15~h on
512 A100 GPUs, and 8~h on 256 H100 GPUs~\cite{bonev2025fourcastnet3}.
These resources are reported separately from inference, and different
accelerator types are not converted into a common cost unit. The
single-member inference timings in Table~\ref{tab:large_weather_context}
are neither full-ensemble timings nor measurements on the hardware used here.
CLCRN-AGF is therefore positioned as a compact setting for retraining and
controlled methodological studies; this experiment does not establish
competitive operational forecast skill or superior speed at matched
resolution and forecast coverage.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The evidence supports a modest relative-humidity improvement from AGF under this fixed protocol, with lower MAE and RMSE after the eight-test correction. It does not support a general improvement across weather targets. The temperature, wind and cloud-cover estimates remain inconclusive, and the broad temperature and wind intervals leave room for both benefit and harm.

The results now address the corrected optimization protocol directly: all
primary and mean-fusion models are newly trained with zero-inclusive
training and validation, saved every epoch and selected by validation alone.
This resolves the earlier training/evaluation support mismatch in the
reported accuracy study. It does not guarantee that AGF improves a given
target. Negative and inconclusive outcomes are retained in the same tables.

The gate combines graph and residual projections of a common pre-CLConv
input. Neither its learned edges nor its scalar coefficients are identified
as meteorological teleconnections, moisture transport or causal contribution.
The component comparison is precision matched, but only three seeds and two
tasks are available for the mean-fusion ablation. Missing-input diagnostics
use a sampled test subset and should not be generalized to other types of
missingness. No new season-stratified experiment is claimed.

Five independently initialized runs per variant on one chronological split
give limited precision and no uncertainty assessment across different climate
periods. These seed-level intervals do not treat individual correlated grid points or forecast hours as independent experimental replicates. The fixed test split was used in earlier development and revision work; the new seeds therefore do not constitute an evaluation on a newly reserved test period. The coarse grid, separate task targets and 12-hour horizon remain
distinct from modern multivariate medium-range forecasting. Contemporary
models require harmonized data, compute and verification before a direct
accuracy ranking is meaningful. Dense graph construction limits scaling,
and the historical-weight microbenchmark is not a new-weight runtime study.


\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
In this controlled experiment, AGF lowers relative-humidity MAE and RMSE by 2.38\% and 3.29\%, respectively, with both comparisons meeting the eight-test BH threshold. No difference is established for the other six primary comparisons. The result is a task-specific finding about training-seed variability on the existing chronological split.
The completed 40-run primary study and six precision-matched ablations provide a traceable test of an incremental AGF input augmentation. The revised claims follow the observed effects, marginal uncertainty intervals and multiplicity-adjusted tests; they do not assert architectural priority for the individual AGF ingredients or parity with global weather models.
